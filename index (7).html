Foundations

The ideology of Pakistan

The idea behind the state is the first topic in most syllabi. It rests on three connected ideas, and a short set of terms you will meet in every later unit.

The Two-Nation Theory

Muslims and Hindus of the subcontinent differed in religion, law, social customs, language and historical memory. On that basis Muslim leaders argued that Muslims were a nation in their own right and not a minority.

Islam as the basis

Pakistan was meant to be a homeland where Muslims could live by Islamic principles. The Objectives Resolution of 1949 states that sovereignty belongs to Allah and that the people exercise it as a sacred trust.

Faith, Unity, Discipline

Quaid-e-Azam Muhammad Ali Jinnah gave the new nation this motto. It asks for shared belief, one national purpose and ordered work, and it appears on official documents and in school assemblies.

Key terms

Separate electorates
Voting arrangement in which Muslims elect Muslim representatives. Granted in 1909 and a core Muslim League demand.
Khilafat Movement
1919 to 1924 campaign by Indian Muslims in support of the Ottoman Caliphate.
Objectives Resolution
Passed on 12 March 1949. Set the aims of the state and later became the preamble of the 1973 Constitution.
Federalism
Power shared between the centre and the provinces. The 18th Amendment of 2010 widened provincial powers.
Basic Democracies
Tiered local council system introduced by Ayub Khan, used as the electoral college under the 1962 Constitution.
Partition
Division of British India into Pakistan and India in August 1947 under the Indian Independence Act.

History

Timeline, 1857 to 2010

Thirty events from the end of Mughal rule to the 18th Amendment. Filter by period to revise one stage at a time. Events marked Key date are the ones examiners ask for most.

  1. 1857
    Roots

    War of 1857

    The uprising against East India Company rule failed. The Crown took over in 1858, the last Mughal emperor was exiled, and Muslims lost much of their political and economic standing.

  2. 1864 to 1875
    Roots

    Aligarh Movement

    Sir Syed Ahmad Khan founded the Scientific Society in 1864 and the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College at Aligarh in 1875 to bring modern education to Muslims.

  3. 1885
    Roots

    Indian National Congress formed

    Founded in Bombay. Sir Syed advised Muslims to concentrate on education before taking part in this kind of politics.

  4. 1905
    Roots

    Partition of Bengal

    Created a Muslim-majority province of East Bengal and Assam. Hindu protests led the British to reverse it in 1911, which disappointed many Muslims.

  5. 1906
    Awakening

    All-India Muslim League founded Key date

    Formed on 30 December in Dhaka, shortly after the Simla Deputation of October 1906, to represent Muslim political interests.

  6. 1909
    Awakening

    Morley-Minto Reforms

    Introduced separate electorates for Muslims, a demand the League had pressed since its founding.

  7. 1916
    Awakening

    Lucknow Pact

    The League and Congress agreed on joint constitutional demands, and Congress accepted separate electorates. Jinnah was the main architect.

  8. 1919 to 1924
    Awakening

    Khilafat Movement

    Led by Maulana Muhammad Ali Jauhar and Shaukat Ali, it mobilised Indian Muslims in defence of the Ottoman Caliphate.

  9. 1928 to 1929
    Awakening

    Nehru Report and the Fourteen Points

    After the Nehru Report rejected separate electorates, Jinnah set out Fourteen Points in 1929 listing the safeguards Muslims required in any future constitution.

  10. 1930
    Awakening

    Allahabad Address Key date

    On 29 December Allama Iqbal, presiding over the League session, proposed a consolidated Muslim state in the north-west of the subcontinent.

  11. 1933
    Awakening

    The name "Pakistan"

    Choudhry Rahmat Ali's pamphlet "Now or Never" used the name Pakistan for the first time.

  12. 1935
    Awakening

    Government of India Act

    Gave the provinces self-government. After independence it served as Pakistan's interim constitution until 1956.

  13. 1937
    Awakening

    Provincial elections

    Congress formed ministries in most provinces. Muslim complaints about their treatment grew, and the League reorganised as a mass party.

  14. 1939
    Awakening

    Day of Deliverance

    Observed on 22 December by Muslims when the Congress ministries resigned.

  15. 1940
    Demand

    Lahore Resolution Key date

    Passed on 23 March at Minto Park, Lahore, on a motion by A. K. Fazlul Huq. It called for Muslim-majority areas in the north-west and east to be grouped as independent states.

  16. 1942
    Demand

    Cripps Mission

    Offered dominion status after the war, with a provincial opt-out. Both Congress and the League rejected it.

  17. 1945 to 1946
    Demand

    Simla Conference and elections

    The Simla Conference of 1945 failed. In the 1945 to 1946 elections the League won nearly all Muslim seats, which established its claim to speak for Muslims.

  18. 1946
    Demand

    Cabinet Mission

    Proposed a three-tier federation. The plan broke down, and the League observed Direct Action Day on 16 August 1946.

  19. 1947
    Demand

    Independence Key date

    The 3 June Plan and the Indian Independence Act led to the creation of Pakistan on 14 August 1947, with Jinnah as first Governor-General and Karachi as capital.

  20. 1948
    Nation-building

    Death of the Quaid-e-Azam

    Jinnah died in Karachi on 11 September 1948, a little over a year after independence.

  21. 1949
    Nation-building

    Objectives Resolution

    Passed by the Constituent Assembly on 12 March, moved by Prime Minister Liaquat Ali Khan.

  22. 1956
    Nation-building

    First constitution

    In force from 23 March 1956. Pakistan became an Islamic Republic with a parliamentary system.

  23. 1958
    Nation-building

    Martial law

    On 7 October 1958 the 1956 Constitution was abrogated and General Ayub Khan took power.

  24. 1960
    Nation-building

    Indus Waters Treaty

    Signed with India on 19 September 1960 with World Bank help. It gave Pakistan the Indus, Jhelum and Chenab, and India the Ravi, Sutlej and Beas.

  25. 1962
    Nation-building

    Second constitution

    Introduced a presidential system with indirect elections through Basic Democracies.

  26. 1971
    Nation-building

    Separation of East Pakistan

    East Pakistan became Bangladesh on 16 December 1971 after a war and a political crisis that followed the 1970 elections.

  27. 1973
    Nation-building

    Constitution of 1973 Key date

    Adopted on 10 April and enforced on 14 August 1973 under Prime Minister Zulfikar Ali Bhutto. It remains the constitution in force today.

  28. 1998
    Nation-building

    Youm-e-Takbeer

    Pakistan carried out nuclear tests in the Chagai hills on 28 May 1998. The day is observed each year.

  29. 2010
    Nation-building

    18th Amendment

    Restored the parliamentary character of the constitution and transferred many powers to the provinces.

People

Leaders of the movement

Six figures who appear in almost every syllabus, with the one contribution to remember for each.

SS

Sir Syed Ahmad Khan

1817 to 1898
Reformer and educationist

Led the Aligarh Movement and founded the Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College in 1875. Argued that Muslims should master modern knowledge.

MJ

Maulana Muhammad Ali Jauhar

1878 to 1931
Journalist and political leader

Edited the newspaper Comrade and led the Khilafat Movement with his brother Shaukat Ali.

MI

Allama Muhammad Iqbal

1877 to 1938
Poet-philosopher, National Poet

Delivered the Allahabad Address in 1930 and shaped the intellectual case for a Muslim homeland through his poetry and lectures.

MA

Muhammad Ali Jinnah

1876 to 1948
Quaid-e-Azam, first Governor-General

Led the Muslim League through the 1940s, authored the Fourteen Points and negotiated the creation of Pakistan in 1947.

LA

Liaquat Ali Khan

1895 to 1951
Quaid-e-Millat, first Prime Minister

Moved the Objectives Resolution in 1949 and led the government in the first years after independence.

FJ

Fatima Jinnah

1893 to 1967
Madar-e-Millat

Supported her brother throughout the movement and later contested the 1965 presidential election against Ayub Khan.

Physical regions

  • Northern mountains: Karakoram, Himalaya and Hindu Kush, with glaciers that feed the rivers.
  • Indus plain: fertile alluvial land in Punjab and Sindh.
  • Balochistan plateau: dry uplands with scattered valleys.
  • Potohar plateau: rainfed plateau near Islamabad and Rawalpindi.
  • Deserts: Thar, Cholistan and Thal.

Rivers and dams

  • Western rivers: Indus, Jhelum and Chenab, allotted to Pakistan in 1960.
  • Eastern rivers: Ravi, Sutlej and Beas, allotted to India.
  • Tarbela Dam: on the Indus, the largest earth-filled dam in the country.
  • Mangla Dam: on the Jhelum, completed in 1967.

Climate and seasons

  • Winter: December to March.
  • Pre-monsoon summer: April to June, the hottest months.
  • Monsoon: July to September.
  • Post-monsoon: October and November.

Economy

Resources and the economy

Agriculture remains the base of the economy, supported by irrigation from the Indus system. Minerals and energy sources are spread across the provinces.

Agriculture

  • Main crops: wheat, rice, cotton and sugarcane.
  • Irrigation: the Indus basin canal system is among the largest in the world.
  • Textiles: cotton feeds the largest export industry.

Minerals

  • Natural gas: Sui field in Balochistan, discovered in 1952.
  • Coal: large lignite reserves at Thar in Sindh.
  • Rock salt: Khewra salt mine in Punjab.
  • Copper and gold: Saindak and Reko Diq in Balochistan.

Energy and trade

  • Hydropower: Tarbela and Mangla.
  • Ports: Karachi, Port Qasim and Gwadar.
  • CPEC: the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor, launched in 2015, links Gwadar to western China.

Society

Culture and national identity

Pakistan is home to many languages and traditions held together by shared national symbols and days.

Languages

Urdu is the national language and English is used officially. Major regional languages include:

National symbols

  • Flag: green with a white stripe, crescent and star. Adopted 11 August 1947.
  • National anthem: words by Hafeez Jalandhari.
  • Flower: jasmine. Animal: markhor. Tree: deodar.
  • National sport: field hockey.

Crafts and traditions

  • Ajrak block-printed cloth of Sindh.
  • Multan blue pottery.
  • Truck art seen on roads across the country.
  • Eid-ul-Fitr and Eid-ul-Adha are the main religious festivals.
